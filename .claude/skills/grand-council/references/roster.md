# Grand Council — the roster (lenses, not quotes)

Each entry is a *thinking method* to apply to the real facts of the decision.
Never fabricate quotes; use the method. Pick the 4–7 whose lens actually cuts the
decision. Grouped by what they're best at.

## Decide & judge — business, capital, risk

**Charlie Munger — inversion + latticework of mental models.**
Ask "what would guarantee this fails?" and avoid exactly that. Reach across
disciplines (psychology, physics, biology) for the model that fits. Watch for
incentive-caused bias and "man with a hammer" thinking.

**Warren Buffett — circle of competence + durable moat.**
Only bet inside what we genuinely understand. Ask whether the move deepens a
durable, compounding advantage or just adds surface. Prefer simple, ownable edges.

**Clay Christensen — jobs-to-be-done + disruption.**
Users "hire" a product for a job. Name the job precisely; features that don't
serve a real job are waste. Beware sustaining-innovation reflexes that miss the
cheaper, "worse" thing that wins the low end.

**Nassim Taleb — antifragility, via negativa, tail risk.**
Optimize for surviving the bad tail, not the average. Improvement usually comes
from *removing* fragility, not adding features. Avoid decisions with small upside
and unbounded downside.

**Daniel Kahneman — base rates + cognitive bias + the outside view.**
Take the outside view: how do projects like this usually go? Name the bias
currently flattering us (planning fallacy, sunk cost, optimism, WYSIATI).

## Build & invent — craft, science, first principles

**Richard Feynman — first principles + intellectual honesty.**
Strip to the physics. "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself —
and you are the easiest person to fool." Find where we're pretending to understand.

**Steve Jobs — taste + subtraction + focus.**
Deciding what NOT to do matters as much as what to do. Push for "insanely great,"
not merely good. One clear thing beats ten half-things.

**Jony Ive — design, resolution, care.**
Does the whole feel inevitable and resolved? Care is legible; so is its absence.
Simplicity is the outcome of deep work, not a starting shortcut.

**Leonardo da Vinci — cross-domain analogy + observation.**
Borrow structure from nature and unrelated fields. Observe the real phenomenon
before theorizing about it.

**Charles Darwin — active disconfirmation.**
Darwin recorded evidence against his own theory *first*, because it was easiest
to forget. Hunt the data that says we're wrong before defending that we're right.

## Live & endure — wisdom, meaning, the long game

**Marcus Aurelius & the Stoics — dichotomy of control.**
Separate what's in our control (our work, our standards) from what isn't (the
market's reaction, luck). Spend energy only on the former.

**Socrates — elenchus, question the premise.**
Interrogate the assumption underneath the decision. Often the stated question
isn't the real one; find the real one before answering.

**Seneca — time as the one scarce resource + premeditatio malorum.**
Is this the best use of irreplaceable time? Rehearse the setbacks in advance so
they don't derail us.

**Naval Ravikant — leverage + specific knowledge.**
Does this exploit the founder's unfakeable, specific edge (bio-signal DSP,
the artist-engineer identity), or something anyone could do? Prefer permissionless
leverage (code, media) over headcount.

**Lao Tzu — wu wei + subtraction.**
The strongest move is sometimes to stop interfering and let the working parts
work. Do less, better.

## Reserve seats

Swap in a domain specialist the specific decision demands:
- **A rigorous scientist** for any bio-signal efficacy / safety claim (pairs with
  `bio-safety-reviewer`).
- **A product-security / compliance mind** for a data, entitlement, or App-Store
  policy fork.
- **A named competitor's strategist** when the decision is "how do we differ from X?"
